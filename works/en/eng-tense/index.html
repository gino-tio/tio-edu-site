1. Past continuous

was / were + verb-ing

An action was going on at a given time or over a period in the past: We were travelling in Europe last month. David was reading the whole afternoon.

FormExample
+I/He/She/It was playing; You/We/They were playing
−was not (wasn’t) / were not (weren’t) playing
Yes/No ?Was he running? — Yes, he was. / No, he wasn’t.
Wh- ?What were you doing at 9 p.m.?
Verb-ing form
most verbssing → singing
ends in emake → making
ends in iedie → dying
vowel + consonant (short)run → running; begin → beginning

Tip Usually NOT used with belong, prefer, know, seem.

2. while and when

two long actions: while … while　·　long + short: when

While Leo was watching TV, Rebecca was reading a book.　(two actions at the same time → past continuous for both, usually while)

While/When we were swimming, a shark suddenly appeared.　(past continuous for the longer action, simple past for the shorter action)

Crystal was quarrelling with her brother when their parents came home.

while / when can go with the longer action; use when for the shorter action.

3. Past perfect

had + past participle

The earlier of two past actions: When I arrived, the film had already started.

Time words: already, just, never … before, by the time, before, after.

FormExample
+She had finished.
−She hadn’t finished.
?Had you seen it before?

1. Farming system

Inputs → Processes → Outputs

Physical inputs: sunlight, air temperature, rainfall, seeds, land, soil nutrients

Cultural inputs: capital, fertilizers, pesticides, technology, labour, irrigation system, machines

Processes: ploughing, seeding, irrigating, fertilizing, weeding, harvesting

Useful outputs: farm produce（crops, meat, milk, wool）→ sold at the market → cash

Feedback: the cash earned from selling produce goes back into the farm (to buy cultural inputs).
Cash crop = grown for sale; subsistence crop = grown for the farmer’s own food.

Useless outputs: animal waste, crop residues, pollutants

2025-26 UT: the useless output of a dairy farm = animal waste (milk and meat are useful).

Farming region = an area where farmers grow the same major crops or raise the same livestock using similar methods.
Granary: China’s 2019 grain output was 613.7 million tonnes; Heilongjiang 66.5, Henan 65.2, Shandong 52 (million tonnes). Heilongjiang’s share = 66.5 ÷ 613.7 × 100% ≈ 10.8%.

Climate = average weather of a place over many years; relief = shape of the land (height and steepness). Growing season = the period of a year when crops can be grown; it depends on rainfall and air temperature, and most crops need ≥ 90 days.
Wheat needs at least 6°C and 400 mm. Paddy: 27–32°C (cannot be grown under 12°C), annual rainfall over 1,000 mm. Sheep: −5 to 28°C, 400–800 mm.

Rainfall decreases from south-east to north-west; in deserts crops are grown at oases; where rainfall is low and only grass grows → pastoral. Temperature is higher in the south → longer growing season in the south (Huanan). Relief falls from west to east; the west is mountainous with steep, rugged land and thin soil → pastoral; the east has plains and hills with fertile soil → arable.
Only about 15% of China’s land is good for farming; the best farmland is in the east along river valleys and near the coast.

Wet rice conditions (past papers):

• Air temperature: 27–32°C (QE version: 20–30°C), minimum not lower than 12°C (QE: 10°C)
• Annual rainfall: over 1,000 mm, at least 125 mm in each month of the growing season
• Growing season: at least 90 days
• Grown in flooded fields
• Relief: flat (plains / lowlands / delta); soil: thick and fertile

Huanan Region (Region C): warm all year round; high annual rainfall (>1,400 mm); long growing season (180–365 days); flat land (Zhujiang Delta); thick, fertile soil → favourable for wet rice, which is subsistence farming.

Xibei Region (Region A): minimum temperature −40°C; annual rainfall <600 mm; growing season 0–180 days; mountainous / plateau (Qing Zang Gaoyuan / Himalayas) with rugged relief; thin, infertile soil and easy soil erosion → only suitable for pastoral farming.

Factors affecting the distribution of farming regions in China: relief, rainfall and temperature (all three).
Factors affecting the length of the growing season: rainfall and air temperature (not air pressure).

Favourable for farming: rainfall 1,800 mm ✓; height 2,000 m ✗; growing season 20 days ✗.

6. Hong Kong food supply

The share of local food production is very small

• Most food is imported.
• Largest source of fresh food = China.
• The major source of rice = Thailand.
• Hong Kong does not produce most of the vegetables it consumes.

7. Food demand

Factors affecting food demand

Population size and level of income.
“Local industrial production” is not a factor affecting food demand.

Population: China’s population rose from 830 million (1970) to 1,400 million (2019) → food demand rises.
Income: with higher income people eat more meat and dairy but fewer staple foods (per capita 1990→2019: grains 131→111 kg ↓; meat 25→36 kg ↑; milk 5→17 kg ↑, the biggest rise).

8. Food supply and self-sufficiency

Food demand > food supply = food shortage

Sources of food supply: ① local production from farming ② imports.
Consequences of food shortage: hunger and poor nutrition → slows economic development → may even cause social unrest. A stable food supply supports economic growth.

China 2000–2018: demand > supply in 2000–2004; supply > demand since 2006. Most grain (rice, wheat, corn) is produced locally and imports are a very small share.
The self-sufficiency rate of grains is about 95% → China generally produces enough food; population is projected to fall from 2025, so the rate will stay high.
